## How to read this

**The baseline is a plan a person wrote**, in the same vocabulary, run by the same executor.
So the only difference between the columns is who chose the actions. A baseline written as
Playwright code would also differ in how it waits and how it locates, and the table would be
comparing two things at once.

5 of the scenarios have a real defect in the application that they are meant to catch, and they run twice: once against the sound application, where passing is correct, and once with the defect switched on, where **passing is a false positive**. The other 2 only run against the sound application, and measure the opposite mistake.

The asymmetry between the two mistakes is the whole point of measuring. A false negative costs
a person an afternoon. A false positive converts an absence of testing into an assurance of
quality, which is worse than having no tests at all, because somebody then ships on it.

**"Detected nothing" is a third outcome, and it exists because the first real measurement
needed it.** A plan that fails the broken shop *and* the sound one has not found the defect;
it fails at everything, and one of the things it fails at happens to be broken. Counting
those as correct verdicts turned "one defect genuinely caught out of six" into "five of six",
which is the sort of number a benchmark exists to prevent.

**A plan that covers part of a scenario and passes that part is inconclusive, not passed.**
The planner says how much it covered, in a field rather than in prose, and a partial plan
whose every step passes ends as inconclusive. The first real measurement had plans that
honestly reported testing half of what was asked and were then counted as passes, which is
an assurance wider than the evidence — the same mistake as a false positive, arrived at
politely.

Flake is measured across repeated **executions** of the same plan, so it describes the
application and our runner, not the model's variation between answers. That is a different
question and it is not answered here.

- **loyalty-card-invoice-adds-up**: The crawl did not include the INV-1019 page itself. The plan reaches it by clicking its link in the Sent list, where the crawl found it. It then uses the test IDs (line-quantity, line-unit-price, vat, total) that the crawled invoice pages INV-1001, INV-1004 and INV-1016 share.
- **loyalty-card-invoice-adds-up**: The Sent list shows INV-1019 with a total of £84.00, which agrees with the expected total.
- **loyalty-card-invoice-adds-up**: The step “Wait for the INV-1019 invoice page to load” acts on heading “Invoice INV-1019”, which was not on any page the crawl saw. That is normal for something that appears only once the test has acted, and a mistake otherwise.
- **clients-page-gives-bluebells-address**: The plan opens /clients directly and checks the name and email elements for Bluebell Bakery using their test ids.
- **paid-filter-shows-the-seven-paid**: The plan starts at /invoices and uses the Paid filter link (filter-paid) rather than navigating directly to a status URL.
- **paid-filter-shows-the-seven-paid**: The count is checked through the invoice-count text 'Showing 7 of 7 invoices'; the crawl showed this wording on the paid filter page for the unmodified data.
- **paid-filter-shows-the-seven-paid**: INV-1005 has status Sent in the crawl, so its row and its link should be absent under the Paid filter.
- **paid-filter-shows-the-seven-paid**: The count assertion assumes the invoice-count text reads 'Showing 7 of 7 invoices' under the Paid filter. That follows the pattern of 'Showing 10 of 25 invoices' and 'Showing 0 of 0 invoices', but the crawl never saw the Paid-filtered page.
