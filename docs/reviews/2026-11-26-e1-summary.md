## The objective

Screens for what the backend already had: members and invitations, roles, the audit log, usage against limits, and
the organization with its plan, read-only. No new permission and no new rule.

## Who sees what

| Page | Who | Enforced by |
|---|---|---|
| Organization | Owners, admins | `IOrganizationService` (`ManageOrganization`) |
| Members | Owners, admins | `IMembershipService`, `IInvitationService` (`ManagePeople`) |
| Audit log | Owners, admins | `IAuditLog` (`ReadAuditLog`) |
| Usage | Everyone in the organization | `IUsageService` (`Read`) |

In every case the service decides. The navigation item carries the same permission (`NavItem.RequiredPermission`,
new in E1.1), so nobody is offered a page that would refuse them. A browser test signs in as a viewer for each
restricted page and finds neither the navigation item nor the content.

## Verification

- **Each part:**
  - service tests with the permission and tenant cases, and four roles refused where the page is restricted;
  - bUnit tests, including German;
  - browser tests, and a smoke test with an axe scan in light and dark themes;
  - a look in a browser, at desktop width and at 390 px.
- **The full suite on E1.3 plus E1.4:** 2,282 of 2,282, zero build warnings.

## Found and fixed while building

- **Audit log:** two filters changed quickly reached the circuit's database context together, and the page showed
  "could not be read". Reads are now one at a time. The browser test that found it ran five times green after the
  fix.
- **Audit log:** the table's sideways-scrolling region could not be reached by keyboard; the accessibility scan
  caught it. It is now a named, focusable region.
